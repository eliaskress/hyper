## 1. Product Vision

Hyper is the influence engine.

Hyper converts influence into a quantifiable economic unit called HI (Hyper Influence). Restaurants buy influence. Creators generate influence. AI agents allocate and measure influence. The system rewards everyone proportionally.

The long-term objective is to build the **Influence Graph** - a foundational dataset mapping how influence propagates across creators, audiences, and merchants. Comparable foundational datasets include those built by Google (intent graph), Meta (social graph), Uber (mobility graph), and Airbnb (supply graph).

Hyper is not an influencer marketing platform. It is an **influence exchange** powered by AI - where influence supply and demand meet continuously.

## 3. Core Metric - HI (Hyper Influence)

HI measures the attention influence generated by content.

### Formula

```
HI = 100 x (L + 2C + 6S + 8SH) / R
```

Where:
- L = Likes
- C = Comments
- S = Saves
- SH = Shares
- R = Reach (unique users who saw the post)

### Measurement Rules

- Reach is measured **24 hours after posting** (70-90% of engagement happens in first 24h)
- If reach is missing, HI cannot be calculated
- Signals prioritize propagation (shares, saves) over passive engagement (likes)

### Platform Reach Proxies

| Platform | Reach metric |
|---|---|
| Instagram | Accounts Reached |
| TikTok | Video Views (closest proxy) |
| YouTube Shorts | Views |

### User-Facing Explanation

Restaurants see: "HI measures real influence. Shares and saves count more because they spread your restaurant to new people."

The raw formula is available behind an expandable "How HI works" section.

## 5. Amplification System

Creators can amplify other creators' campaign posts. This is the foundation of the Influence Graph.

### Rules

- Amplifier reward: 30% of original HI
- Max 5 amplifiers per post
- Amplification cannot exceed 50% of creator's own HI in that campaign
- Amplification rewards are paid from the amplification pool, never creating new HI

### Why Amplification Matters

Amplification is not just a feature - it is the mechanism that makes the Influence Graph visible. When creators amplify each other, Hyper learns who moves influence. Over time the system discovers influence leaders based on propagation power, not follower count.

## 6. Creator Reputation - HIG (Hyper Influence Grade)

Each creator receives a HIG score (0-100).

### Components

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| HI performance | 50% |
| Amplification effectiveness | 20% |
| Reliability | 20% |
| Network contribution | 10% |

### Reliability Score

Based on: post completion, deadline adherence, post deletions.

### Impact

Higher HIG = priority access to higher-budget campaigns and better amplification opportunities. HIG should feel like earned credibility, not a gamified treadmill.

## 7. Campaign Lifecycle

### States

```
draft -> active -> creator_assigned -> content_posted -> metrics_pending -> completed -> paid
```

### Creator Assignment States

```
invited -> accepted -> scheduled -> posted -> measured -> paid
```

Also: `declined` (creator rejects match)

### Response Window

Matched creators have **72 hours** to respond before the match expires. This is the default for all campaigns.

### Flow

1. Restaurant sets budget (in HI units) and content brief
2. AI allocates creators based on geography, HIG, reliability, platform relevance
3. Creators receive match notification (WhatsApp + email)
4. Creator accepts and selects posting platforms
5. Creator schedules visit and posts content
6. Creator sends post link
7. After 24 hours, metrics are extracted
8. HI calculated, payout created
9. Both sides receive summary

## 10. The Influence Graph

The Influence Graph is the core moat. Each event becomes a graph edge.

### Nodes

- Creators
- Restaurants
- Posts
- Campaigns

### Edges

- Amplification relationships
- Engagement propagation
- Campaign participation

### What It Reveals Over Time

- **Creator Propagation Score**: How often creators trigger other creators
- **Local Influence Clusters**: Which creators move the same audiences
- **Restaurant Viral Probability**: Which restaurants will propagate across creators
- **Predictive Intelligence**: Who will influence what, before it happens

### Creator Clusters

A creator cluster is 5-20 creators who frequently influence each other within a geographic area. Clusters are the real unit of value - when a cluster activates around a restaurant, it creates a cascade (10+ posts, viral moment) rather than a single post.

## 11. Geographic Expansion Framework

### Geo Cells

Expansion operates through neighborhood cells, not cities.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| neighborhood | Name (e.g. Silver Lake) |
| radius_km | Typically 2-2.5 km |
| target_creators | ~60 |
| target_restaurants | ~10-12 |
| status | seed / growing / dense / expand_ready |

### LA Launch Order

| Priority | Neighborhood | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Silver Lake / Echo Park | Highest micro-creator density, indie restaurants, active food posting culture |
| 2 | West Hollywood | Social restaurants, nightlife influence, higher budgets, strong amplification chains |
| 3 | Venice / Abbot Kinney | Tourism + lifestyle, high foot traffic, fast restaurant ROI proof |

### Density Thresholds

When a cell reaches ~10 restaurants + ~60 creators, it becomes self-sustaining. Creators refer other creators, restaurants see competitor activity, and the influence loop closes.

### Key Metric: HI per square mile

If this number grows in a neighborhood, Hyper is working. If it doesn't, density is insufficient.

## 12. Restaurant Experience - Design Philosophy

The restaurant UI should feel like **watching influence spread**, not campaign tracking.

### Core Principles

- Show **influence propagation**, not just post metrics
- Show **movement** (rising/stable/slowing), never just numbers
- Make the Influence Graph visible through data, even without a map visualization

### Dashboard Cards

1. **Influence Spread** (hero metric): Creators posted / Amplifications / Secondary creators. Subtitle: "Your influence is spreading beyond your creators."
2. **Monthly Spend + ROI**: Budget spent, estimated reach, estimated visits
3. **Trend Score**: "Rising in West Hollywood" with directional arrow
4. **Creator Schedule**: Upcoming visits (day, date, handle, time)
5. **Creator Impact Ranking**: Creators sorted by total HI, with neighborhood, posts, amplifications

### What Restaurants Actually Want

Restaurants don't want influencer marketing. They want their restaurant to become **the place everyone posts**. The UI should visually show that moment happening.

## 13. Creator Experience - Design Philosophy

The creator UI should feel like an **influence operating system**, not a job board.

### Core Principles

- Influence first, money second. Lead with HI generated, then show earnings.
- Creators should never feel idle - there must always be something to do (campaigns, amplification, network growth)
- Propagation must be visible: who amplified you, whose posts you could amplify

### Navigation

```
Home | Campaigns | Influence | Earnings | Profile
```

Not: Home | Collabs | Earnings | Referrals | Profile

### Key Screens

1. **Home**: Your Influence (HI generated), estimated earnings, upcoming visits, influence momentum this week
2. **Campaigns**: New Matches (invited assignments with countdown), My Campaigns (accepted/completed), Open Opportunities
3. **Influence**: Your influence stats, propagation (who amplified you), amplification opportunities, your network, HIG rank
4. **Earnings**: Transparent breakdown ($10/HI x 40% = $4/HI), payout history with statuses
5. **Profile**: HIG score, platform links, badges, email preferences

### Always-On Influence (Critical)

Creators must never feel they are waiting for campaigns. The system must provide:
- **Open Opportunities**: Restaurants currently rewarding influence, no invitation required
- **Amplification Feed**: Other creators' posts you can amplify for HI
- **Influence Alerts**: Notifications about nearby opportunities

Without this, Hyper becomes a job board and creators churn.

## 14. Creator Drops (10x Growth Feature)

Drops are micro-events where multiple creators visit the same restaurant in a time window.

### How It Works

1. Restaurant sets: Drop time + creator slots
2. Nearby creators receive alert
3. Creators claim spots
4. 5-6 creators visit together, generating 12-18 pieces of content
5. Creators tag each other, amplify each other's posts
6. Creates a propagation burst

### Why Drops Matter

- Creators love going where other creators are (social proof, content opportunities, meeting peers)
- Drops create clean propagation data for the Influence Graph
- Restaurants get predictable creator moments instead of waiting for random posts
- Organic creator acquisition: "Hey I'm going to a Hyper drop tonight" -> friend joins
- Solves cold start: even with 5 restaurants and 20 creators, you can run 3 drops/week

## 15. Email Notification System

### Creator Emails

| Email | Trigger |
|---|---|
| New match | Assignment created with status "invited" |
| Expiring match | 12 hours or less remaining on response window (daily cron) |
| Match accepted confirmation | Creator accepts assignment |
| Daily digest | Morning summary: today's visits + pending matches (daily cron) |
| Payment received | Payout marked as paid |

### Restaurant Emails

| Email | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Creator accepted | Creator accepts an assignment |
| Creator scheduled visit | Creator schedules a visit date |
| Creator posted | Creator submits post link |
| HI results | Post measured, HI score calculated |
| Weekly campaign summary | Monday morning performance report (weekly cron) |

### Preferences

Users can toggle each email type on/off from their profile. Preferences stored as JSONB on the users table. All send functions check preferences before dispatching.

## 17. Market Formation Agents (Autonomous Growth)

Two AI agents continuously build geographic density.

### Influencer Acquisition Agent (IAA)

- Scans: Instagram location tags, TikTok location search, YouTube Shorts location content
- Target: micro-creators (1,000-50,000 followers)
- Cadence: every 6 hours
- Limit: max 40 outreach/day per geo cell
- Channels: Instagram DM, TikTok DM, email
- Positive response triggers WhatsApp onboarding

### Restaurant Acquisition Agent (RAA)

- Scans: Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram business profiles
- Prioritizes: independent restaurants, visual food categories, active Instagram, rating > 4.2
- Cadence: every 12 hours
- Channels: Instagram DM, email, website form, WhatsApp
- Positive response triggers restaurant onboarding

### Density Monitoring

Track per geo cell: creator_count, restaurant_count, campaign_count, weekly_hi.

When density threshold reached (10 restaurants, 60 creators), status moves to "dense" and agents shift focus to the next neighborhood.

## 18. The Influence Density Loop

The core growth flywheel:

```
Restaurant campaign
-> creators generate content
-> content drives customers
-> restaurant sees results
-> restaurant increases budget
-> creators earn money
-> creators invite other creators
-> more creators generate influence
-> restaurants see more reach
-> more restaurants join
```

### Three structural conditions required:

1. **Geographic density**: Campaigns within 5-10 minutes so creators can stack them ($50-$150/day)
2. **Creator earnings visibility**: "Creators near you earned $4,320 this week"
3. **Restaurant social proof**: "82,000 local impressions this week in your neighborhood"

### The signal it's working

Creators start saying: "Hyper paid for my dinner tonight" or "Just did two Hyper collabs in one afternoon." At that moment, recruitment becomes automatic.
